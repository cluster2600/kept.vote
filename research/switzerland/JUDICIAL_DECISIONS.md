# Judicial decisions and serious offences after release

Initial source review: **23 October 2026**. The working window for recent decisions
is **2019–2026**; a later judgment can describe earlier events. The four selected
records in `judicial_decision_reviews.json` are publication drafts, not a ranking.

A useful accountability record distinguishes the original sentence, pre-trial
release, sentence completion, parole, prison leave, compulsory treatment,
expulsion/residence decisions and subsequent offences. Different institutions may
control each step. A later federal appeal panel is not automatically the body that
authorised the earlier release.

## Concrete findings

**Sentencing reasoning, 2023–2024.** In 7B_15/2021 / 7B_16/2021 (19 September 2023),
the Federal Supreme Court panel consisted of Sonja Koch (presiding), Christoph
Hurni and Yann-Eric Hofmann. A passage accepting consideration of a rape's short
duration was expressly disavowed in 6B_612/2024 (18 September 2024). This is a
specific, documented criticism of reasoning. The original opinion is collegial;
no identified individual author, dissent, disciplinary violation or later homicide
is established. Separately, the 2023 panel overturned aspects of the cantonal
sentence; do not confuse those grounds with the 2024 clarification.

**Morges attack, 2020; oversight report, 2023.** The AB-BA considered conditional
release from pre-trial custody defensible but criticised the federal prosecution's
reaction to breaches and its risk assessment/coordination. This is not a finding
against a named releasing judge. The subsequent conviction and the earlier
release/monitoring decision have separate sources and procedural roles.

**Basel Nasenweg homicide, 2024; review, 2025.** The report concerns leave during
institutional treatment, involving UPK and SMV. It records a failure to recognise
a particular risk but also correct decision processes and no serious professional
misconduct. Do not convert those nuanced findings into an accusation that a judge
freed someone unlawfully. Offender identity and nationality are not reconstructed
from redacted material.

**Zurich homicide, 2021; judgment, 2025.** 6B_298/2025 establishes an offence after
the unsuspended part of an earlier sentence had been served. The person's Turkish
nationality is explicit in the judgment. The earlier sentencing panel has now been recovered (see below); the
release/enforcement record remains outstanding. The judges hearing the 2025
appeal cannot be labelled the judges who released him in 2021.

Full source URLs, follow-up gaps and qualified summaries are in the JSON records.
No individual judge has been found culpable in this initial review. This does not
establish that no culpability was found in other records or later proceedings.

## What a defensible comparison would require

“Most absurd” is an opinion, not a statistical variable. Publish the exact
reasoning, any appellate criticism or legal violation, and subsequent disposition
instead of an unsupported superlative. An overturned decision does not by itself
establish professional misconduct.

For a comparison of release decisions followed by serious offending, obtain a
systematic cohort of decisions with court/panel identities, decision type,
prosecution requests, available risk evidence, offender eligibility, nationalities
as explicitly documented, follow-up periods and subsequent final convictions.
Include all eligible decisions and their caseload denominators, not only cases
that made the news. Count collegial decisions transparently; do not guess the
individual vote or assign every decision to its chair.

No nationwide judge-linked release/reconviction dataset was obtained in this
review. Public statistics by canton or nationality are not sufficient to rank
individual judges. There is no verified answer here to “which judge released the
most foreign nationals who subsequently killed someone.” Unknowns remain null,
and no nationality is inferred from a name, language, appearance or immigration
headline. These four cases are not a representative nationality comparison.

## Original 2021 Basel judgment now located

A search in the court's public interface for **SB / 2021 / 9** returns two
separate decisions. **AG.2021.589** is the original judgment dated 30 July 2021,
first published 17 November 2021 and updated 23 November 2023. The archived copy
is the current court-published text, not a verified first-publication snapshot.
Its panel was **Liselotte Henz (chair), Lucienne Renaud and Carl Gustav Mez**.
It imposed **36 months, 18 suspended, and six years' expulsion**. This differs
from the recovered 2024 panel and its later sentence. The original decision
explicitly identifies Portuguese nationality (section 11.2); it does not establish
a later homicide. Its written reasoning acknowledges possible severe trauma
from brief assaults and disclaims victim contributory blame, while still using
duration and other circumstances in sentencing. A separate release order is
not contained in the recovered dispositive text.

The Morges case also has a later compensation judgment, **A-2081/2025, 13 October
2025**. The Federal Administrative Court rejected the claimant's appeal because
she did not meet the applicable close-relative/partner requirement. This does not
determine fault in the earlier release or monitoring decisions. Subsequent finality
remains unverified.
[Compensation judgment](https://entscheidsuche.ch/dok/CH_BVGer/CH_BVGE_001_A-2081-2025_2025-10-13.pdf).
